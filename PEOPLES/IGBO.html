In Igbo and other Eastern Forest societies, music is used widely—at royal honors, assemblies, funerals, festivals and storytelling—and often involves masked performance and satirical topical songs; In Igbo society, music making is not a class or profession: individuals specialize according to talent and act as interpreters of the community’s music; training is informal, mainly by imitation, reflecting decentralized, non-urban political organization. Musical traits show influences from Central Africa: equal prominence of membranophones (igba) and slit-drums (ekwe, ufie), rich percussive instruments (drums, bells, rattles, percussion vessels, wooden clappers), and melodic instruments (xylophones, lamellophones, flutes, trumpets, musical bows, pluriarcs).
