Their music, representing one of the largest groups in Sierra Leone, shows a dual character. On one hand it shares Western Forest cluster traits such as prominent percussion, masked dancers and secret-society music. On the other hand it incorporates features associated with the Western Sudanic cluster: occasional use of the fiddle (named angbulu, gbulu, rafon in Temne) and a tendency of women to imitate a Sudanic singing style. These Western Sudanic traits are said to have been adopted through interaction with Fulɓe during Temne territorial expansion east and northeast, and through Fulɓe dispersal in Sierra Leone.
