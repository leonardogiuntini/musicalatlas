Before about 1400, groups in this area, particularly those in Liberia, Sierra Leone and west Côte d’Ivoire, felt little influence from the savanna empires of Ghana and Mali; this isolation allowed the development of small, widely scattered states. With the disintegration of the Mali Empire in the 1400s, Malinke traders and warriors moved from the savanna into the kola plantations of the forest, bringing merchandise and Islam, affecting areas in and around Liberia.

WOLOF are described as a fusion of Serer, Mande and Fulɓe elements. Their ancestors occupied the southern area of present-day Mauritania, cohabiting with Fulɓe; in the 1300s they developed their Jolof states, and by about 1450 their kingdom’s fiefs stretched from the Senegal River to the Gambia River.

TEMNE migrated from the Futa Jallon region of Guinea, who left their original settlements to escape FULƁE invasions in the 15th century, and migrated south before settling between the Kolenté and Rokel River area of Sierra Leone. Their music, representing one of the largest groups in Sierra Leone, shows a dual character. On one hand it shares Western Forest cluster traits such as prominent percussion, masked dancers and secret-society music. On the other hand it incorporates features associated with the Western Sudanic cluster: occasional use of the fiddle (named angbulu, gbulu, rafon in Temne) and a tendency of women to imitate a Sudanic singing style. These Western Sudanic traits are said to have been adopted through interaction with Fulɓe during Temne territorial expansion east and northeast, and through Fulɓe dispersal in Sierra Leone.
