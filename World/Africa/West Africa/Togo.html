Togo straddles both savanna (north) and forest/coastal (south) zones of West Africa, so its musical life reflects both savanna traditions (Voltaic and Central Sudanic influences, Islamic-linked professional specialists) and forest/coastal traditions (Aja/Ewe microstates, elaborate court and vodun ceremonies, masquerades, and dense dance-club networks.
Among the EWE of Togo, regional differences in scale and harmony persist: northern Ewe music favors a seven-tone diatonic system with polyphony in thirds (borrowed from neighboring AKAN), while southern ANLO-EWE use a five-tone scale with harmony in parallel fourths and a generally lower vocal tessitura.

Northern GUMBAY music and traditional southern AGBADJA are identified as long-standing traditions that feed into new styles like GOUMBEJA; their existence is presented as pre-dating contemporary RAP / REGGAE experiments.

- 1967–1973: President Gnassingbé Eyadéma is described as a “tyrant” whose 38 years of undivided rule, where music has “not had a high profile” in Togo, are only ending with his recent death (2005). Togo is represented on high-profile West African and international stages by Bella Bellow, whose career marks an early modern Togolese presence in the wider region; she becomes a model for emotive, romance-focused popular song, inspiring a succession of Togolese female singers: Yta Jourias, Mabah, Afia Mala.

- Mid-1980s: In the Togolese capital Lomé, the Africa New Sound studio operates as one of Africa’s most sophisticated 24-track recording facilities, attracting artists such as Abeti Masikini and Dr. Nico (both from Congo).

- early 1990s–c. 2000: “Social unrest and military violence” shake Togo, particularly affecting youth; people fear holding parties at home because of militia punitive expeditions. The situation “hinders the careers” of talented musicians on the national scene, prompting many to move to Europe, or across the border to Benin or Cameroon. Jimi Hope creates his own “heady mix of rock and tropical rhythms,” coupled with lyrics that openly denounce abuses of power. Fifi Rafiatou gains some international success singing in IFE and Ewe dialects.

- 2001–2004: - King Mensah AFRO-BEAT to REGGAE spectrum – his music is “driven by powerful percussion deeply rooted in African traditions”. Field recordings of Kabiyé Orchestras and Lithophones (Ewe traditional music) in northern Togo document agricultural-year music and instruments, indicating parallel attention to heritage while youth scenes grow. 2003: Launch of the Togo Hip-Hop Awards ceremony. December 2004: Second staging of the Africarap Festival, backing the awards and signalling institutional support for HIP-HOP (Dzoku-Kay, Djantakan, Small Poppy, Eric M.C., Wedy, RX Patou).
