Niger described as a huge landlocked nation merging with the Sahara to the north. Niger’s “diverse culture” described in terms of major regional groupings (Touareg north; Hausa centre/south; Beriberi east; Djerma/Songhai west; Dendi south).Wodaabe Fulani “gerewol” face-painted dancing and polyphonic singing ceremony for young men described as an important annual date on Niger’s calendar.

Dendi described as using techniques/instruments from neighbouring Benin and Nigeria.

Beriberi “beautiful polyphonic singing”.

“Wonderful historical recordings” made made by french composer and ethnomusicologist Tolia Nikiprowetsky in Niger in 1963 with Songhai, Djerma, Hausa, Beriberi, Touareg and Fulani musicians.

“Belluwel” and “akhaguwwen” singing techniques documented in recordings made 1973–1998 in northwest Niger (Azawagh region).

Mystical chant “dhikr” documented around In Gall (recordings dated 1973–1998).

“Sahel drought of 1973” described as leaving enduring hardship and halting Niger’s small recording output. In “strongly Muslim Niger,” music as entertainment “not readily accepted”; for many years after independence there was little cultural policy.

Electronic Nigerien music from Mamman Sani.

After the death of military dictator Seyni Kounché (1987), government looked to music to bring cultures together. Prix Dan Gourmou launched (cash prize + national youth-club tour), prompting music/dance ensembles, with some groups seek to add modern instruments to traditional ones. Alassane Dante (formerly director of the National Ballet; co-director of CCFN) among those who set up Prix Dan Gourmou.

Abdallah Oumbadougou, beacon TOUAREG guitarist for ISHUMAR / DESERT-BLUES style, formed the band Takrist n'Akal and later participated in the international band Desert Rebelle.

Mamane Barka (nomad of the Toubou tribe from north), Groupe Oyiwane (northern Niger collective)

Centre for Musical Training and Promotion established for modern techniques/instruments; materialized 1990 via a six-year European Fund for Development grant. Takeda, leaded by guitarist Abdoulaye Alhassane Toure (Koudede) and formed with top CFPM musicians and singers: Yacouba Moumouni, Moussa Poussy (Djerma; grandson of a traditional healer), Adams Junior, Fati Mariko, John Sofakolé played MASA 1995 in Abidjan.

Niamey described as having only two small studios (incl. CFPM) and only two tape-duplicating companies; musicians often self-produce cassettes, facing piracy.

Saadou Bori (Hausa-speaker; practitioner of dango spirit music and trance-dancing).

First RAP concert in Niamey at CCFN (1996). Nigerien rap follows the exiled Belgian-based group Tod One; early groups include Massacreur and Wassika (first to write lyrics in Hausa and Djerma). Lilwal (collective formed 2001) bringing together Black Daps, Djoro G, WassWong, Kaïdan Gaskia. Other Nigerien rappers: Sah Fonda, MTS Matassa, Metaphore Crew.

Modern Nigerien group Mamar Kassey described as inspired by Fulani, Hausa, Songhai, and Djerma traditional music.

Festival-driven “hip” attention to Touareg music “in recent years”; first Festival in the Desert (2001) referenced as a globalizing catalyst for Malian band Tinariwen and desert sounds, carrying “desert pride” message worldwide; 2003 festival recording described as a classic. Tidawt (from Agadez); Etran Finatawa: (four Touareg + six Wodaabe) trance-like rock guitars + driving percussion + chanting; songs about healing, herding cattle, camel racing; some built on calabash patterns with call-and-response vocals.

Dangana (formed 2001 in Zinder) highlighted for forging a new sound via terracotta jars and a hybrid rock-like approach.

Radio Ténéré’s support for Lakal Kaney.

Harouna Goge described as a virtuoso of the one-stringed goge fiddle (following his father, though against his wishes); performs Dendi-country songs (mostly spirit invocations).

Mdou Moctar described as the "Hendrix of the Sahara". Bombino.
